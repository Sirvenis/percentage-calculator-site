How to Calculate Percentages

Understanding percentages is essential for everyday life, from calculating tips at restaurants to determining discounts while shopping. A percentage is simply a way of expressing a number as a fraction of 100. The word "percent" comes from the Latin "per centum," meaning "by the hundred."

The Basic Formula

The fundamental formula for calculating a percentage of a number is: Percentage × Number ÷ 100 = Result. For example, to find 20% of 150, you calculate 20 × 150 ÷ 100 = 30. This means 20% of 150 equals 30.

Finding What Percentage One Number Is of Another

To determine what percentage X is of Y, divide X by Y and multiply by 100: (X ÷ Y) × 100 = Percentage. For instance, if you scored 45 out of 60 on a test, your percentage is (45 ÷ 60) × 100 = 75%.

Calculating Percentage Change

Percentage increase or decrease measures how much a value has changed relative to its original amount. The formula is: ((New Value - Old Value) ÷ Old Value) × 100. If your rent increased from $1,000 to $1,200, the percentage increase is ((1200 - 1000) ÷ 1000) × 100 = 20%.

Practical Applications

Percentages are used everywhere: sales tax calculations, interest rates on loans and savings, nutritional information on food labels, survey results, and statistical data. Percentage vs Percentage Points

Many people confuse percentages with percentage points, but they represent different concepts. Understanding the distinction is crucial for accurate interpretation of data and statistics.

What Are Percentage Points?

A percentage point is the simple arithmetic difference between two percentages. If an unemployment rate rises from 5% to 8%, that's a 3 percentage point increase (8% - 5% = 3 percentage points).

What Is a Percentage Change?

A percentage change (or relative change) measures the change relative to the original value. In the same example, the unemployment rate increased by 60% relative to the original rate ((8 - 5) ÷ 5 × 100 = 60%).

Why the Distinction Matters

Confusing these terms can lead to significant misinterpretation. A headline saying "Unemployment rises by 60%" sounds much more alarming than "Unemployment rises by 3 percentage points," even though both statements describe the same change. Always check whether a report uses percentage points or percentage change to avoid drawing incorrect conclusions.

Example

If a company's market share grows from 20% to 25%, that's a 5 percentage point increase, but a 25% relative increase ((25-20)/20 × 100 = 25%). Both figures are correct—they just describe the change differently.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?
To calculate a percentage of a number, multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For example, 20% of 150 = 150 × 20 ÷ 100 = 30. You can also move the decimal point two places to the left on the percentage and multiply: 150 × 0.20 = 30.
What is the formula for percentage increase?
Percentage increase = ((New Value - Original Value) / Original Value) × 100. For example, if a price goes from $80 to $100, the increase is ((100-80)/80) × 100 = 25%. A positive result indicates an increase, while a negative result indicates a decrease.
How do I calculate a tip?
Multiply your bill amount by the tip percentage and divide by 100. To split the total (bill + tip) among people, divide by the number of people. For example, a $50 bill with 20% tip split between 2 people: Tip = $50 × 20 ÷ 100 = $10, Total = $60, Per person = $30.
What is the difference between percentage and percentage points?
A percentage point is the arithmetic difference between two percentages. If something increases from 10% to 15%, that is a 5 percentage point increase, but a 50% relative increase. Percentage points measure absolute differences, while percentages measure relative changes.
How do I calculate a discount?
Multiply the original price by the discount percentage and divide by 100 to find the discount amount. Subtract this from the original price to get the final price. For example, a $80 item with 25% off: Discount = $80 × 25 ÷ 100 = $20, Final price = $80 - $20 = $60.

What is a good tip percentage?
In the United States, 15-20% is customary for restaurant service. 15% for adequate service, 18% for good service, and 20% or more for excellent service. For delivery, 10-15% is common. Tipping customs vary by country, so research local norms when traveling abroad.
