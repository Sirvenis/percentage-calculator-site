Percentage vs Percentage Points: What's the Difference?

Published: April 2026 | Reading time: 5 min

Confusing "percentage" with "percentage points" is one of the most common mathematical mistakes — and it can lead to seriously wrong conclusions. Let's clear this up.

Percentages Measure Relative Change

A percentage describes a proportion relative to a whole. When we say something increased by 50%, we mean it grew to 150% of its original value. If your salary was $40,000 and increased by 50%, your new salary is $60,000.

Percentage Points Measure Absolute Difference

Percentage points describe the simple arithmetic difference between two percentages. If an interest rate goes from 3% to 5%, that's a 2 percentage point increase. But it's NOT a 2% increase — it's actually a 66.7% increase ((5-3)/3 × 100).

A Real-World Example

Say a company's market share goes from 20% to 25%:

  • The increase in percentage points is 5 (25 - 20 = 5)
  • The increase in percent is 25% ((25-20)/20 × 100)

Both are correct, but they describe different things. The percentage point change tells you the raw difference. The percentage change tells you the relative growth.

Why This Matters

In Finance

"Interest rates rose by 2%" sounds small. But if rates went from 3% to 5%, that's actually a 66.7% increase in the cost of borrowing. Media headlines often misuse these terms, creating confusion.

In Medicine

"The drug reduces risk by 50%" could mean the risk dropped from 2% to 1% (a 1 percentage point decrease, which IS a 50% relative reduction). Context is crucial.

In Politics

Poll results: "Support rose from 40% to 45%." This is a 5 percentage point increase, but only a 12.5% relative increase. Politicians often choose whichever framing sounds more impressive.

The Rule of Thumb

When you see a percentage change mentioned, always ask: percentage of what? If it's the difference between two percentages, it should be expressed in percentage points. If it's the relative growth of a single value, it's a percentage change.
