Understanding Percentage Increase and Decrease

Published: April 2026 | Reading time: 6 min

Percentage increases and decreases appear everywhere — salary negotiations, sale prices, investment returns, and statistics. Understanding how to calculate them correctly is essential.

The Formula

Both percentage increase and decrease use the same formula:
Percentage Change = ((New Value - Original Value) / Original Value) × 100

A positive result means an increase. A negative result means a decrease.

Percentage Increase Examples

Salary Raise

Your salary increased from $50,000 to $55,000. ((55,000 - 50,000) / 50,000) × 100 = 10% raise.

Population Growth

A city's population grew from 250,000 to 275,000. ((275,000 - 250,000) / 250,000) × 100 = 10% growth.

Investment Returns

Your portfolio went from $10,000 to $12,500. ((12,500 - 10,000) / 10,000) × 100 = 25% return.

Percentage Decrease Examples

Discount

A jacket originally cost $120 and is now $84. ((120 - 84) / 120) × 100 = 30% discount.

Weight Loss

Someone went from 200 lbs to 180 lbs. ((200 - 180) / 200) × 100 = 10% weight reduction.

Revenue Decline

Quarterly revenue dropped from $500K to $425K. ((500,000 - 425,000) / 500,000) × 100 = 15% decline.

Common Mistakes

  • Wrong denominator: Always divide by the ORIGINAL value, not the new one
  • Confusing percentage points with percentages: A change from 10% to 15% is a 5 percentage point increase, but a 50% relative increase
  • Assuming symmetry: A 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease does NOT return to the original. $100 → +50% = $150 → -50% = $75

Reverse Percentage Calculation

If you know the percentage change and final value, you can find the original. Example: After a 20% increase, a price is $150. Original = $150 / 1.20 = $125.
